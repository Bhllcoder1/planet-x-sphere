### Overview

|  |
| --- |
| SPHEREx is a NASA Astrophysics Medium Explorer mission that launched in March 2025. During its planned two-year mission, SPHEREx is obtaining spectroscopy from 0.75 to 5.0 um across the entire sky, with deeper observations in the SPHEREx Deep Fields. IRSA releases Quick Release spectral images on a weekly basis, with additional releases of [higher-level data products](https://spherex.caltech.edu/page/data-products) planned throughout the mission and beyond.    IRSA began releasing SPHEREx spectral image data on a weekly basis in July 2025 (Quick Release 1; QR1). In October 2025, IRSA transitioned to releasing data processed with substantially improved calibrations (Quick Release 2; QR2), with reprocessed versions of all QR1 images provided. QR1 was formally retired in February 2026 and is no longer available from IRSA. In April 2026, the QR2 headers were updated to correct [PSF extension header errors](/data/SPHEREx/docs/psfhdrerr.html). In September 2026, IRSA began releasing Quick Release 3 (QR3) data, which incorporate improved PSF and other calibrations for more reliable photometry. In November 2026, IRSA released [Data Release 1 (DR1)](/data/SPHEREx/docs/overview_dr1.html), which reprocesses the QR2 data so that the DR1 and QR3 spectral images together form a uniformly processed set of spectral images. With the release of DR1, QR2 data are no longer returned in IRSA or VO searches, but remain available for direct download through February 2027.    If you use SPHEREx QR data from the IRSA archive, please cite the appropriate Digital Object Identifier: [10.26131/IRSA629](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/doi/irsa/10.26131/IRSA629) for QR1, [10.26131/IRSA652](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/doi/irsa/10.26131/IRSA652) for QR2, [10.26131/IRSA662](https://www.ipac.caltech.edu/doi/10.26131/IRSA662) for QR3, and include the following acknowledgement:    "This publication makes use of data products from the Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization and Ices Explorer (SPHEREx), which is a joint project of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory and the California Institute of Technology, and is funded by the National Aeronautics and Space Administration." |
